This study examines the territorial and temporal distribution of investments
associated with sustainable rural credit in Brazil and evaluates their
correspondence with productive-territorial and environmental characteristics
across the 27 Federative Units from 2013 to 2024. Following the CRISP-DM
framework, a balanced panel of 324 FU--year observations was analyzed using
descriptive and concentration measures, within--between decomposition,
extensive- and intensive-margin models, robustness specifications, and a
Territorial Alignment Indicator (IAT). The results show that the recovery in
real investment after the mid-2010s contraction was not accompanied by a
comparable recovery in the number of credit operations, indicating increased
financial intensity per operation. Persistent differences across FUs were
generally more informative than short-term within-FU variation, particularly
for positive investment amounts. The IAT further shows that the territorial
distribution of investment was consistently more closely aligned with land-use
shares than with GHG-emission shares. Although the magnitude of national
alignment varied with the weighting of these dimensions, 77.5\% of FU--year
observations retained the same relative discrepancy position across the full
weighting interval. These findings demonstrate that financial scale, credit
occurrence, territorial concentration, and distributive alignment capture
distinct dimensions of sustainable rural-credit allocation. The study provides
a reproducible framework for territorial diagnosis while interpreting
alignment strictly as distributive correspondence rather than as evidence of
causal impact, policy effectiveness, or optimal resource allocation.
